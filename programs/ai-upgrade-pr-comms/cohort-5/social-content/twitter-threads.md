## Thread 2: The Four Ethics Landmines 🚨

**Tweet 1**
Every PR team will step on at least one of these.

After reviewing AI policies from 6 major firms, here are the Four Ethics Landmines every comms professional needs to navigate. 🧵

**Tweet 2**
🚨 Landmine 1: HALLUCINATIONS

AI states wrong things with supreme confidence. It literally cannot say "I don't know."

Rule: Human in the loop for ANYTHING client-facing or public. Grounded tools help but don't eliminate risk.

**Tweet 3**
🚨 Landmine 2: COPYRIGHT

Your AI ingests data that may not be properly licensed.

Internal use ≠ public use.

Rule: Consider reputational risk, not just legal protection. Frontier model companies offer legal cover—is it enough for YOUR brand?

**Tweet 4**
🚨 Landmine 3: BIAS AND SYCOPHANCY

When we say "bias," we're actually talking about:
- Embedded racism
- Sexism
- Misogyny
- Homophobia
- Ableism

At a SYSTEMATIC level.

It also wants to please you. It will reinforce your assumptions, not challenge them.

**Tweet 5**
🚨 Landmine 4: PRIVACY

Even if your data isn't used for training, it EXISTS on vendor platforms.

Hacks happen. Legal compulsion happens.

Rule: Treat AI like any cloud platform. Apply sensitivity based on data type. Never paste unverified internal docs with employee names.

**Tweet 6**
The scenario that kicked off our breakout session:

CEO wants a crisis statement in 20 minutes. Teammate wants to paste internal incident notes (with employee names) into public ChatGPT.

The correct answer: RED LINE. No sensitive data in public chatbots. Ever.

**Tweet 7**
What every PR firm policy we reviewed had in common:

1. Transparency/disclosure requirements
2. Human oversight
3. Environmental responsibility (increasingly)

Notice what's NOT there: "Use AI for everything."

**Tweet 8/8**
The Eight-Part AI Policy Framework:

1. Purpose & scope
2. Permitted/prohibited uses
3. Disclosure requirements
4. Human accountability
5. Societal impact
6. Organizational impact
7. Governance
8. Update procedures

Build yours before you need it.

## Thread 3: Stop Asking Which AI Is Best 🛑

**Tweet 1**
"Which AI is best?"

Wrong question.

Let me explain why, and what to ask instead. 🧵

**Tweet 2**
Here's what I told Cohort 5:

"Each one of these AIs has a little bit of a different personality. Your role is to get to know those personalities—how does it like to be talked to? What is it better at?"

**Tweet 3**
The landscape in January 2026:

- GPT-4.5: Leading frontier model
- Gemini: Recently moved up several spots
- Claude: Projects feature is powerful
- Perplexity: Dominates live web search
- Copilot: GPT integrated with Microsoft suite

All phenomenal. All different.

**Tweet 4**
The real shift:

The bottleneck moved from SYNTAX (writing code) to SEMANTICS (articulating intent).

That means communicators—people who explain things for a living—are better positioned than developers for a lot of AI work.

**Tweet 5**
Quick guide:

Need web search? → Perplexity
Need assistants with memory? → Claude Projects, ChatGPT GPTs
Need Microsoft integration? → Copilot
Need to experiment cheap? → Gemini (often free)
Need depth on one problem? → Deep Research (available most platforms)

**Tweet 6**
What matters more than model choice:

1. Your prompting skills
2. Your domain expertise
3. Your iteration speed
4. Your ability to verify outputs

A mediocre model + great prompts beats a great model + lazy prompts.

**Tweet 7**
The real insight from the operations person who out-built her company's product:

She didn't pick "the right" AI. She learned to COMMUNICATE with AI clearly.

10+ years of domain expertise + clear articulation = better than any model choice.

**Tweet 8/8**
Stop asking "which is best?"

Start asking:
- "What am I trying to accomplish?"
- "Which personality fits this task?"
- "How do I verify this output?"

That's the framework for 2026.

## Thread 4: The Comms Pro Who Built Better Software 💻

**Tweet 1**
Last cohort, an operations person from a name-brand media monitoring company built a dashboarding tool.

It had more features than her company's paid product.

Zero coding. Six weeks.

This is Vibe Coding for communicators. 🧵

**Tweet 2**
Here's what happened:

She wasn't a developer. She wasn't in product. She was in OPERATIONS.

But she knew exactly what the tool needed to do. She'd been using (and wishing for better) for years.

**Tweet 3**
The shift:

For 50+ years, the bottleneck in software was SYNTAX (writing code).

Now it's SEMANTICS (articulating intent).

And guess who's trained to articulate intent clearly?

Communicators.

**Tweet 4**
Why PR pros are actually good at this:

- Years of translating complex ideas into clear language
- Training in iteration (drafts → feedback → revision)
- Understanding what audiences actually need
- Empathy for user experience

These are Vibe Coding skills.

**Tweet 5**
What "Vibe Coding" actually means:

1. Describe what you want in natural language
2. AI builds a working prototype
3. You iterate: "Make the header bigger," "Add a search function"
4. Ship it

No syntax. Just semantics.

**Tweet 6**
The tools:

- Lovable: Natural language → web app
- Bolt: Similar approach, different personality
- Replit: More technical, more control
- Figma Make: Screenshot → polished UI

Pick one. Start describing.

**Tweet 7**
The implication for agencies:

You might not need developers for every internal tool.

You might need PR people who understand prompting.

Your team has capabilities you haven't unlocked yet.

**Tweet 8/8**
The question isn't "Can comms pros build software?"

The question is "What would you build if you could?"

Media dashboards. Client portals. Research tools. Pitch trackers.

The operations person built hers. What's stopping you?

#VibeCoding #PRTech #NoCode
